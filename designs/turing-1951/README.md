# turing-1951

> **"At some stage therefore we should have to expect the machines to take
> control…"** — Alan Turing, 1951. The founder of computer science said it
> 75 years ago, in a talk called *Intelligent Machinery, A Heretical
> Theory*. It's set as a justified poster stack, every line the same width,
> with **THE MACHINES / TO TAKE CONTROL** in the accent colour. Along the
> bottom runs a strip of 1950s five-hole paper tape (the medium of the
> Manchester computers Turing programmed). Its holes spell **PAUSE AI** in
> the ITA2 teleprinter code of the time, an easter egg for anyone who
> decodes it. The point is simple: this worry isn't new, fringe or
> sci-fi. It's as old as the field.

## Source (verified 2026-09-28)

- **Text:** A. M. Turing, "Intelligent Machinery, A Heretical Theory"
  (c. 1951), in B. J. Copeland (ed.), *The Essential Turing*, Oxford
  University Press, 2004, ch. 12, p. 475. We checked it against the
  chapter's full text: https://gwern.net/doc/ai/1951-turing.pdf
  - Publisher's page: https://academic.oup.com/book/42030/chapter/355746549
  - Typescripts: King's College Cambridge, Turing Digital Archive, AMT/B/4:
    https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/publications-lectures-and-talks-amtb/amt-b-4
- **Full passage** (the printed words are in bold):
  > "…for it seems probable that once the machine thinking method had
  > started, it would not take long to outstrip our feeble powers. There
  > would be no question of the machines dying, and they would be able to
  > converse with each other to sharpen their wits. **At some stage
  > therefore we should have to expect the machines to take control**, in
  > the way that is mentioned in Samuel Butler's 'Erewhon'."
- **Truncation:** we print the sentence up to "take control" and mark the cut
  with "…". The dropped tail (", in the way that is mentioned in Samuel
  Butler's 'Erewhon'") is a literary reference that doesn't change the
  meaning.
- **Date / venue:** the archive and Copeland both give only **c. 1951**, and
  no exact day is known. The King's archive describes it as "a lecture
  delivered to the '51 Society at Manchester". Copeland describes The '51
  Society as a BBC radio discussion programme recorded in Manchester. The
  print says "ALAN TURING, 1951" and cites "c. 1951, talk for The '51
  Society, Manchester".
- **Spanish rendering:** «En algún momento, por tanto, deberíamos esperar que
  las máquinas tomen el control…» is our translation. The ES print labels it
  "traducción PauseAI en Español", and the English original is kept here.
- **The tape:** decorative. The holes encode "PAUSE AI " (repeated) in ITA2
  (International Telegraph Alphabet No. 2), one column per character: five
  data holes plus the small feed (sprocket) hole. It's a stylised rendering,
  not a byte-accurate reproduction of Mark 1 tape.
