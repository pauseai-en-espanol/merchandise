# the-last-garden

**ES:** El último jardín · *formerly `el-ultimo-jardin`*

> A small planet, entirely tiled in **solar panels and data centres**,
> except one last patch of ground on top where a single person stands
> beside a single tree. The planet sits on the left; to its right, Ilya
> Sutskever's own words are set as a CAIS-style packed block (every row the

> words alone carry the claim.

## Source (verified 2026-09-30)

- **Quote:** *"I think it's pretty likely the entire surface of the earth
  will be covered with solar panels and data centres."*
- **Where:** "Ilya: the AI scientist shaping the world", **The Guardian**,
  published **2 November 2023**, at **10:21**:
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9iqn1HhFJ6c (official Guardian channel).
  Checked against the Guardian's own English (en-GB) captions. The auto
  captions agree.
- **Context (verbatim, the lines just before):** *"…the way I imagine it is
  that there is an avalanche, like there is an avalanche of AGI development.
  Imagine it, this huge unstoppable force. And I think it's pretty likely…"*.
  He is describing a race between teams building AGI. Our framing (no
  room left for us) is consistent with that; we don't add words.
- **Footage origin:** the interview material was filmed for the documentary
  *iHuman* (dir. Tonje Hessen Schei, 2019), which is why the line is often
  cited as "iHuman, 2019". We cite the Guardian film because it's the
  publicly available source we checked.
- **Edit:** we print from "I think…"; the spoken sentence begins "And I
  think…". Spelling "centres" follows the Guardian's captions.
- **Spanish rendering:** «Creo que es bastante probable que toda la
  superficie de la Tierra acabe cubierta de paneles solares y centros de
  datos.» is our translation.
- **Who:** Ilya Sutskever, OpenAI co-founder and former chief scientist; in
  2024 he founded Safe Superintelligence Inc. No likeness is used.

## How the art is made

`scripts/draw-planet.py`: pure vector, stdlib only. It uses an orthographic
projection of a latitude/longitude grid, seen from slightly above. Each cell
is either a solar panel (2 × 3 panel cells with real gaps) or a data-centre
block with status LEDs (17 %, fixed seed). The polar cap is the garden. Tune
`NLAT`/`NLON`, `TILT`, `CAP_LAT`, `DC_SHARE` there and re-run. The quote is set
in `TXT[lang]['rows']` as a packed block, like `cais-extinction`. A `fill` row
is one line stretched to the block width; a `rot` row sets a short connector
rotated 90° (reading upward, as tall as the big word's cap height) followed
by the big word filling the rest. The block shrinks and centres vertically if
it would overflow `TOP`..`BOTTOM`. Closing punctuation is set at `PUNCT` × the
line's size so it doesn't shout.
